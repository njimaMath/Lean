Blueprint: proof that B'(t) = 𝔼[g(U_t)]

Settings
--------
Fix κ ∈ ℝ and t ∈ (0,1). Let Z ~ N(0,1), and write 𝔼[·] for expectation with
respect to Z.

Definitions (matching main.tex)
-------------------------------
1) Standard normal quantities:
   - φ(u) := (1/√(2π)) exp(-u^2/2)
   - Φ(u) := ∫_{-∞}^u φ(s) ds
   - Φ̄(u) := 1 - Φ(u) = ∫_u^∞ φ(s) ds

2) Inverse Mills ratio:
   - E(u) := φ(u) / Φ̄(u).

3) Random change of variables:
   - Z ~ N(0,1)
   - U_t := (κ - √t Z) / √(1 - t).

4) The function B:
   - B(t) := (1 - t) · 𝔼[ E(U_t)^2 ].

5) Auxiliary functions:
   - h(u) := E(u)^2
   - g(u) := E(u)^2\bigl(3E(u)^2-4u\,E(u)+u^2-2\bigr).


Dependencies / facts used
-------------------------
A) Differentiation facts for the inverse Mills ratio (from differentiating E(u)=φ/Φ̄):
   - φ'(u) = -u φ(u), and Φ̄'(u) = -φ(u).
   - E'(u) = E(u)^2 - u E(u).
   - E''(u) = 2 E(u)E'(u) - E(u) - u E'(u).

B) Gaussian integration by parts (IBP):
   If Z ~ N(0,1) and ψ is smooth with suitable integrability, then
     𝔼[ Z ψ(Z) ] = 𝔼[ ψ'(Z) ].

C) Justification step (analytic):
   To differentiate B(t) under the expectation, show an integrable domination for
   the relevant random variables (e.g. h(U_t), h'(U_t)∂_t U_t). In main.tex this is
   handled using growth bounds on E(u) (Mills bound) plus Gaussian moments.


Goal
----
For t in (0,1), prove the derivative identity

  B'(t) = 𝔼[g(U_t)].


Proof steps
-----------
Step 1: Rewrite B(t) with h.
  B(t) = (1 - t) · 𝔼[ h(U_t) ].

Step 2: Differentiate B(t).
  Using the product rule and differentiation under the expectation,
    B'(t) = -𝔼[h(U_t)] + (1 - t) · 𝔼[ h'(U_t) · ∂_t U_t ].

Step 3: Compute ∂_t U_t explicitly.
  U_t = (κ - √t Z) / √(1 - t).
  A direct calculation gives
    ∂_t U_t = U_t / (2(1 - t)) - Z / (2√t √(1 - t)).

  Substitute this into Step 2 and simplify:
    B'(t) = 𝔼[ -h(U_t) + (1/2) U_t h'(U_t) ]
            - ( √(1 - t) / (2√t) ) · 𝔼[ Z h'(U_t) ].

Step 4: Eliminate the Z-term by Gaussian IBP.
  Define, for z in ℝ,
    ψ(z) := h'( (κ - √t z)/√(1 - t) ) = h'(U_t(z)).

  Then d/dz U_t(z) = -√( t / (1 - t) ), so by the chain rule,
    ψ'(z) = -√( t / (1 - t) ) · h''(U_t(z)).

  Apply IBP (Fact B):
    𝔼[ Z h'(U_t) ] = 𝔼[ Z ψ(Z) ] = 𝔼[ ψ'(Z) ]
                   = -√( t / (1 - t) ) · 𝔼[ h''(U_t) ].

  Substitute back into Step 3:
    -( √(1 - t) / (2√t) ) · 𝔼[ Z h'(U_t) ]
      = +(1/2) · 𝔼[ h''(U_t) ].

  Therefore,
    B'(t) = 𝔼[ -h(U_t) + (1/2) U_t h'(U_t) + (1/2) h''(U_t) ].

Step 5: Express h', h'' in terms of E, E', E''.
  Since h(u) = E(u)^2,
    h'(u) = 2 E(u)E'(u),
    h''(u) = 2(E'(u))^2 + 2E(u)E''(u).

  Substitute into the formula from Step 4 to get
    B'(t) = 𝔼[ -(E(U_t))^2
               + U_t E(U_t)E'(U_t)
               + (E'(U_t))^2
               + E(U_t)E''(U_t) ].

Step 6: Use the identity for E'' to simplify the combination
        U_t E E' + E E'' - E^2.
  With u = U_t and E = E(u), E' = E'(u), E'' = E''(u),
  plug Fact A (E''(u) = 2EE' - E - uE') into:
    uEE' + EE'' - E^2
      = uEE' + E(2EE' - E - uE') - E^2
      = 2E^2E' - 2E^2
      = 2E^2 (E' - 1).

  Hence the integrand in Step 5 becomes
    (E')^2 + 2E^2(E' - 1).

  Therefore,
    B'(t) = 𝔼[ (E'(U_t))^2 + 2E(U_t)^2 (E'(U_t) - 1) ].

Step 7: Identify the integrand as g(U_t).
  By definition of g(u),
    g(u) = E'(u)^2 + 2E(u)^2(E'(u) - 1) = E'(u)^2 - 2(1 - E'(u))E(u)^2.

  So the identity from Step 6 is exactly
    B'(t) = 𝔼[ g(U_t) ].


End of blueprint.
